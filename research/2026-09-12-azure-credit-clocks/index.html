Azure’s USD 1,000 startup credit has a 90-day clock. The student route does not.

Pool topic: Global Credits & Grants Radar
Question revision: 1
Question used: Which credits and grants are currently open, who is eligible, what do they actually provide, and what are the requirements and deadlines?

Microsoft currently presents two different Azure routes that can look interchangeable in a list of “free credits.” They are not. One is for eligible full-time university students; the other is for a new Azure customer building a startup. Their qualification gates and expiry clocks create different decisions.

For students: USD 100, up to 12 months

Azure for Students offers USD 100 in Azure credit to use within the first 12 months, with no card required at sign-up. Microsoft says the applicant must be 18 or older, a full-time student at an accredited degree-granting two- or four-year institution, and able to verify academic status with an organisation email. The offer is one subscription per eligible customer and cannot be combined with another offer unless Microsoft permits it.

This is not a general startup budget. The offer details frame the benefits for education, non-commercial research, and developing or demonstrating software; Azure Marketplace and several separately licensed products are excluded. When the credit is exhausted, the subscription is disabled unless the reader moves to a paid route.

For new startups: USD 1,000 first, then a short verification window

Azure’s current account comparison says Azure for Startups gives a new Azure customer USD 1,000 in credits instantly without an application. Business verification can raise the total to USD 5,000. The initial credit is valid for 90 days; post-verification credit is valid for 180 days. A card is required for identity verification and services beyond the credit, and pay-as-you-go pricing follows expiry or use.

The practical question is not “Can we get USD 1,000?” It is “Can our planned workload use it inside 90 days, and can we complete business verification before that window closes?” The reviewed comparison does not publish country coverage, detailed business-verification criteria, or a complete service-exclusion list, so those remain checks before a team commits an architecture.

Why a bundle headline is not a term

GitHub’s Student Developer Pack terms make the wider lesson explicit: partner benefits have their own terms, may not stack, and can change. A recent community post questions whether a partner credit still kept the advertised duration after a change. That report does not establish Azure terms or an outcome; it is a reason to record the issuer, exact route, expiry, and cost transition instead of treating a “student pack” or “startup credit” label as a promise.

What remains unknown

We have not confirmed Azure for Startups country availability, detailed business-verification requirements, or all eligible services. We also do not treat the Azure for Students offer as evidence of a worldwide individual-credit route. Check the official source immediately before sign-up.
